[Maeumjigi Capstone Dev Log #15] Visualizing Fine-Tuning Results of 5 Models & Local Environment Setup

May 7, 2026 · 2 min read
jbgih
Antigravity Developer

May 7th was the day we synthesized the fine-tuning results of our 5 models (Llama3.3-70B, DSR1-70B, HCX-Omni-8B, Qwen35-9B, HCX-Text-1.5B) and proceeded with full-scale visualization and local environment setup.

Visualization and Local Setup for Model Comparison​

To comfortably review the result comparison code (which was previously running on the heavy server) on my personal laptop, I created a new notebook, 31_comparison_local.ipynb.

"Can't 30_comparison.ipynb run on my laptop too?" "Please tell me how to install pandas, matplotlib, seaborn, and bert_score on the currently selected kernel."

While setting up the local Jupyter kernel in VSCode and installing the necessary libraries, I faced frequent Kernel Crashes and annoying errors like ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'ft_utils'. However, I resolved them step-by-step by fixing folder paths. I also successfully recovered the missing ft_metrics.json files for some models (like DSR1-70B) using a script.

5-Model Comprehensive Report and Graph Visualization​

The most important task was visualizing the performance of these 5 models so they could be compared at a glance. Together with the AI, we established a plan named 5MODELS_ORGANIZATION_PLAN_present.md and began systematically organizing the data.

"What is the difference between the faint line and the thick line? Bring the training loss and eval loss for each model and visualize them as graphs." "Remove the thick line and make the faint line clearer and save it."

I removed the unnecessary trend lines (thick lines) from the matplotlib graphs generated by the AI, finally extracting 03_training_loss_all.png and 04_eval_loss_all.png which neatly gathered the Training and Eval Loss curves of all models in one place. We also perfectly documented graphs analyzing the LoRA Rank Decomposition for each model, as well as a GPU memory usage chart (02_gpu_memory.png).

Now, based on these graphs, we can beautifully decorate our capstone design mid-term presentation materials for next week. Which of these 5 models will ultimately be selected as the best quality 'Maeumjigi' chatbot?

[Maeumjigi Capstone Dev Log #13] Limits of 70B Training and the Pivot to Mid-Size Models (HCX, Qwen)

May 4, 2026 · 2 min read
jbgih
Antigravity Developer

After experiencing the OOM (Out of Memory) hell during the fine-tuning of massive 70B models, on May 4th, we attempted a bold change in strategy. By drastically reducing the batch size, we barely managed to push the 70B models (Llama3.3, DeepSeek-R1) to checkpoint-1200 and 1300, but the training time was absurdly long, resulting in terrible efficiency.

Pivoting to Mid-Size Models (1.5B ~ 9B)​

Considering our physical server resources and the capstone project deadline, we decided it was too risky to rely solely on the 70B models. Therefore, we shifted our focus to relatively lighter mid-size models like HCX-Omni-8B, Qwen35-9B, and HCX-Text-1.5B.

"It's taking a long time to train HCX-Omni-8B, Qwen35-9B, and HCX-Text-1p5B with the current hyperparameters. Are these parameters generally used for these models?" "How does increasing the batch size affect the training?"

Things weren't perfectly smooth just because the models were smaller. To speed up training, we increased the batch size and endlessly debated with the AI to find the optimal hyperparameters for each model size. We first evaluated the training results of the smallest model, HCX-Text-1.5B, to set our tuning direction.

Checkpoint Saving Errors and Kernel Version Issues​

While smoothly training HCX-Omni-8B, we hit another snag while trying to save a checkpoint at step 50.

[RANK 0] Detected kernel version 5.4.0, which is below the recommended minimum of 5.5.0; this can cause the process to hang.
...
![사진 02](./images/02_error.png)

JSONDecodeError Traceback (most recent call last)
---> 60 trainer.train(resume_from_checkpoint=...)

Along with a warning that the OS kernel version was too low and could cause the process to hang, we faced a horrific JSONDecodeError because the saved checkpoint file was corrupted, preventing us from resuming training.

"If the models stop during training, do they resume from where they stopped?"

Fortunately, the Hugging Face Trainer provides a feature to resume training from checkpoints (resume_from_checkpoint), but it's useless if the checkpoint file itself is corrupted. Despite reducing the model size, we spent the entire day wrestling with numerous framework and infrastructure errors. Will we ever reach a stable training orbit?
